# A Mathematical Treatise on Praxeology

## Purpose and audience

This book develops a mathematical account of action under physical constraints, uncertainty, and social interaction. It begins with agents, means, ends, and feasible choices, then builds toward exchange, money, organization, political institutions, business-cycle models, and the limits of historical inference.

The intended reader knows elementary logic and set theory. Each chapter introduces the additional mathematical ideas it uses, including relations, finite orders, branching histories, and integer-valued quantities. Definitions precede their use. Theorems state their assumptions, and proofs explain each inference.

The book presents conditional mathematical results: each applies to arrangements satisfying its stated conditions. Finite examples illustrate definitions and show that particular assumptions can hold together.

## How to read the arguments

Most arguments keep three kinds of evidence distinct. The **physical and accounting layer** records stocks, production, and transfers along a history. The **epistemic and deliberative layer** records which histories an agent considers plausible, which actions are contemplated and feasible, and which branch represents each alternative. The **evaluative bridge** states how the agent ranks the resulting ends. Physical quantities and arithmetic do not by themselves imply a choice; a theorem that concludes that someone invests, trades, or withholds an offer needs explicit preference and menu premises.

Later applications package related evidence so it can be checked and reused: a credit advance, an inventory trace preserving the principal, a rate comparison, and a completion bottleneck can be stated separately before being combined into a cycle result.

Finite witness worlds serve a distinct mathematical purpose from general conditional theorems. A theorem establishes that a conclusion follows if its premises hold; a concrete finite model (an **action frame witness**) shows that the selected premises can hold together without contradiction or vacuity. In constructive praxeology, these witnesses operate at two levels:
- **Model-Theoretic Consistency:** Constructive models (such as the two-moment frame, bilateral trade frame, workshop hierarchy frame, credit lifecycle frame, and grounded business-cycle frame) certify that the axiomatic system is inhabited and mathematically sound.
- **Mechanism Demonstration:** Concrete multi-agent worlds establish that joint economic phenomena—such as entrepreneurship under uncertainty, capital structure distortion, complementary factor starvation, and error clustering—arise directly from the verified interaction of physical conservation, temporal branching, and subjective valuation.

## Notation and reading conventions

Sets are denoted by capital letters, their members by lower-case letters, and quantities of physical means by natural numbers. A strict preference relation is written $e_1 \succ e_2$ and is interpreted only at the agent and decision context specified in the surrounding definition. Unless a result explicitly assumes additional order properties, asymmetry does not imply transitivity or connectedness.

Displayed equations use standard mathematical notation. Each chapter numbers its definitions and results in order; references are to chapters and numbered results in this book. Technical terms are defined before they are used. A proof may cite an earlier result only after its hypotheses have been checked in the current setting.
