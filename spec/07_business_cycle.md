# Specification 07: Business-Cycle Mechanisms and Remaining Construction

This specification describes the current business-cycle modules: [`Entrepreneurship.lean`](../Praxeology/Entrepreneurship.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Information.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Information.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean), [`BusinessCycle/CreditLifecycleFrame.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/CreditLifecycleFrame.lean), [`BusinessCycle/DefaultWitness.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/DefaultWitness.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Auctions.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Auctions.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Regimes.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Regimes.lean), [`BusinessCycle/FactorExecution.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/FactorExecution.lean), [`BusinessCycle/ProfileProgram.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/ProfileProgram.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Outcome.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Outcome.lean), [`BusinessCycle/BehavioralProgram.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/BehavioralProgram.lean), [`BusinessCycle/BehavioralChoices.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/BehavioralChoices.lean), and [`BusinessCycle/IntegratedResults.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/IntegratedResults.lean). The shared schedule compiler is in [`FiniteEconomy/ProfileSchedule.lean`](../Praxeology/FiniteEconomy/ProfileSchedule.lean).

The results are conditional mechanisms. They do not establish that every credit expansion causes a crisis or that a credit shock alone synchronizes errors across an economy.

## 1. One Entrepreneurial Cycle

`EntrepreneurialCycleScenario` records an entrepreneur's investment, intertemporal rate signals, capital formation, and contextual evaluation. The cycle theorem combines purposeful investment with blocked completion and derives regret from an explicitly feasible safe action, a shared counterfactual context, admitted outcomes, and a valuation comparison. Blockage by itself is insufficient for regret.

`CreditProjectEvidence` packages a cycle with its appraisal and credit-financing menu. Its purposeful-choice theorem uses the existing rate-appraisal and loan-advance conditions. A quoted rate change alone does not determine an entrepreneur's preference or choice.

The finite ActionFrame witness in `BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean` constructs one entrepreneur's matched investment, missing complement, blocked completion, and contextual regret. Its second actor is the exchange counterparty; it is not a two-entrepreneur cluster model.

## 2. Credit Lifecycle Outcomes

`PawnLoanTerms` stores the principal and promised repayment separately from later events. `EnactedLoanQuote` contains the terms and an enacted advance only; it does not require a successful repayment. The successful main history in `CreditLifecycleFrame.lean` contains a purposeful advance and a matched purposeful repayment before its stated maturity. `CreditRepayment` is evidence of that later event; it is not inferred from the contract's promised amount.

`DefaultWitness.lean` reuses history eight of the same finite lifecycle frame. The first advance remains purposeful there by `PurposefulAct.bundle_invariant`, which derives invariance across histories sharing the decision moment and enacted action. A second advance is matched before the first repayment decision. At that repayment decision, the entrepreneur idles while the lender accepts; the acceptance is unmatched, so the loan is not repaid. The branch reaches its declared maturity at the resulting endpoint. The proof rules out a matched physical repayment at every intervening history moment by the enacted actions on the branch. `UnpaidMaturity` derives default status from this evidence. The inventory run records the collateral received in the second advance, proves the lender does not spend collateral before maturity, and derives an aggregate collateral balance at maturity at least as large as the first advance's post-origination balance plus its pledged quantity. `PhysicalSettlementWitness.lean` separately constructs a matched advance and repayment as `AdvanceExecution` and `RepaymentExecution`, without requiring either party's purposeful-action certificate; `CreditAdvance` and `CreditRepayment` retain the stronger behavioral evidence where a theorem needs purposefulness.

The unpaid branch also supplies the enacted quote `entrepreneurUnpaidEnactedLoanQuote`; `entrepreneur_unpaid_quote_has_no_repayment` proves that its advance has no successful-repayment certificate. This exhibits default without making repayment part of origination. This is an aggregate stock result for a fungible good. The ActionFrame does not track the identity or legal title of particular collateral units, or represent a foreclosure judgment, so the result is not reported as foreclosure. This lifecycle witness remains separate from the five-project program; the latter has its own stipulated nonrecourse maturity-settlement stage. `IntegratedResults` separately derives contextual regret within two named five-project default branches. Neither construction models legal foreclosure.

## 3. Saving and Credit Signals

`CreditShockCohort.observation` reads each regime directly from its `ActionFrame` decision node and Catallactics signal reader; its knowledge relation is equality of those derived observations. The module derives market signals from existing data: loan gross repayment factors, prices in enacted clearing actions, and factor-price bounds. One route to equal saving and credit rate signals is `CatallacticCreditRateComparison`: both contracts' gross repayment factors are tied to purposeful clearing prices in the same Catallactic market, with the same borrower and lender and a singleton mutually acceptable reservation interval. `PurposefulFundedClearingRound.same_price_of_singleton_reservation` derives equal clearing prices from the ActionFrame-grounded reservation brackets; `CatallacticCreditRateComparison.same_gross_repayment_factor` then derives equal contract rates. This is conditional on common-market and singleton-reservation evidence, not on regime labels alone. `SavingCreditLoanQuotes` packages the enacted saving-side and credit-side advances, purposeful saving evidence, and this market comparison; it does not require repayment. Separate theorems comparing two contracts from identical enacted advance and repayment offers take actual `CreditRepayment` evidence for each contract. ActionFrame offer injectivity then yields equal principal and repayment quantities; `PawnLoanTerms.same_grossRepaymentFactor_of_same_principal_and_repayment` in `Catallactics/Credit.lean` derives equal gross repayment factors from the loan identity. Those theorems concern repayment observations and do not make repayment part of loan origination. Saving evidence also records purposeful retention of a principal-sized balance and no money spent before the advance; the inventory balance law proves that the balance remains available at the loan. The no-shock quote is tied to the natural rate. The ABCT appraisal inequalities prove that the credit rate is lower than that baseline, and the cohort's rate classifier derives the lower-rate observation. Saving and credit observations are equal by theorem rather than by an equality field, and `market_rate_does_not_identify_credit_regime` derives indistinguishability from that result. `source_disclosure_distinguishes_saving_and_credit` shows that an additional source disclosure distinguishes the regimes even when the market reading alone does not; that disclosure is an explicit extra observation, not inferred from rate equality. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or fully establish funding origin.

`ObservationPolicy.same_observation_same_choice` shows that equal observations yield the same policy response under one fixed policy. The result does not establish that the response is available or executed. The credit-project records provide separate appraisal, funding, menu, feasibility, and execution evidence for the credit decision. In the five-project program, the household cash transfer is an exchange for an identified deposit claim promising three units. The claim and corresponding bank liability are recorded only when the two transfer actions match; redemption is another matched exchange at settlement. `BehavioralChoices.fiveProjectHouseholdSavingAction_purposeful` separately proves purposeful household choice under its stated terminal preference and complete plan menu. This does not show that signal equality caused the transfer or identify the original source of the household's cash.

The named household-saving calibration makes an additional cash assumption: the household starts with two money units and may transfer both to the bank. The fixed example specifies the household's two completion inputs but not this cash endowment. `fiveProjectRegimeDataWithHouseholdMoney` exposes both endowment and transfer as inputs; funding remains conditional on those inputs. The finite plan menu allows an affordable deposit choice, consumption from zero through the factor endowment, and an offer quantity from zero through the unconsumed stock. Unoffered factors remain with the household. The purposeful-choice proof uses the stated household preference over deposit balance, consumed services, money, and retained holdings; it does not derive those preference premises from the physical schedule.

`BusinessCycle.Regimes` gives projected source totals and forecast calculations; `ProfileProgram` independently executes from the reached branch state. In the named baseline, credit-expansion, and household-transfer runs, respectively, the loan quotes are six, four, and four; factor prices are one, two, and one; and projects 0–1, 0–1, and 0–3 complete. Projects 2–3 settle by nonrecourse collateral terms in the credit-expansion run; the other named runs have no default events. The household-transfer run opens and redeems the deposit claim on matched exchanges, ending with household cash five. These are physical and contractual results for the selected schedule, not purposeful-choice conclusions. The separate named run with all initial cash buffers set to four recomputes the market at price three: only projects 0–1 complete, while projects 2–3 repay from cash and retain collateral. Supplying four completion factors instead allows all four funded projects to complete, sell output, and repay. The four-project/two-factor case therefore remains physically bounded even with larger cash; money does not supply the missing inputs.

`ProfileProgram` places the named regime schedule in a fuel-bounded recursive physical program. The finite phase list determines its execution fuel, and `FiveProjectPhysicalCursor.events` is the source for recorded plans, issuance, matched contracts, purchases, completions, sales, repayment, and default settlement. Transfer records retain the enacted profile and proofs that offer and acceptance match. `ProfileSchedule` remains the fixed-schedule specialization. The generated program exposes each listed affordable alternative and retains idling; each branch continues from the state produced by the inventory interpreter. The household plan records deposit, consumption, and offered quantities separately. Consumption service is logged when its operation is enacted; a market offer is capped by remaining holdings, and retaining a factor does not itself offer it. The selected history follows the schedule's selected profile. Separate results prove purposeful household choice and subjective project-two and project-three acceptance under explicit preferences and the four-factor forecast; the latter forecast is not inferred from the actual household plan. `IntegratedResults` proves contextual regret for the actual project-two and project-three default branches relative to declining the loan, using terminal collateral holdings and the stated collateral-first preference. The generator clears loan capacity from bank money after issuance and household transfer, records advances only for matched offers and acceptances, and clears later factor markets from actual advances and currently available offered stock. Market participation preserves the stated initial-buffer rule; each proposed transfer separately passes through current-stock affordability. Clearing terms stay fixed once assigned, and later markets are recomputed when reached, without automatic reauction. Failed transfers consume their cursor slot and are not retried. The five-project run has its own contractual maturity stage: successful repayment returns collateral, while unpaid maturity settles the debt under the stipulated nonrecourse terms. This is distinct from the separate `DefaultWitness` lifecycle path. Neither path represents a foreclosure action. `BusinessCycle.Outcome` projects physical holdings, delivered services, and matched contract, repayment, default, and collateral records into one outcome object.

## 4. Shared Resource Shortages

`at_least_k_projects_unfunded` proves that if accessible supply falls `k` units below the number of unit-demand projects, at least `k` distinct projects receive no input. The cluster result therefore requires a shortfall bound with `k ≥ 2`.

`SharedInputShortage` ties this accounting to a `CreditShockCohort` and ActionFrame balances. Its finite resource-owner set is partitioned into accessible owners and consumers retaining the input; gross supply, retained stock, and accessible stock are sums of their actual balances. The exposed entrepreneurs' balances are a subset of accessible holdings, so the aggregate allocation bound is derived. Each project must require one unit of the shared input, and the projects must share the relevant completion node. The theorem derives blockage for each zero-allocation project, then composes that blockage with the project's own purposeful-choice and contextual-regret evidence.

`SharedInputShortage.contextual_regret_cluster` packages that composition as a finite witness: at least `minimumFailures` failed projects are returned, each with its entrepreneurial-cycle conclusion. Since the structure requires `minimumFailures ≥ 2`, `SharedInputShortage.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` further returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's cycle conclusion.

The declared resource-owner set must cover the relevant pool, including transfers, production, and imports represented in its ActionFrame balances. The theorem concerns completion at the specified node; it does not prove permanent failure.

## 5. Price Squeezes

`CommonFactorPriceResponse` states a shared price-box change: one bottleneck factor's upper price rises by a specified amount, while other upper prices remain fixed. The module proves the resulting increase in completion cost from factor demand.

`PriceSqueezeCohort` connects the price-box response to ActionFrame observations and cash balances. It ties the pre- and post-response factor-price signals to decision nodes on the same history and proves that the bottleneck price signal changes. The price rule derives higher completion cost; the cash-buffer crossing then derives blockage and, with each project's safe alternative and contextual evaluation, regret. Its `minimumFailures` threshold states how many affected projects the scenario requires. `at_least_minimum_failures_unaffordable` and `contextual_regret_cluster` return a finite subset meeting that bound. `completion_feasible_with_sufficient_post_price_cash` gives the boundary case: if an affected project's completion is contemplated, its cash covers the post-response factor bill, and its other inputs are covered, the ActionFrame feasibility law proves that completion remains feasible.

`PriceSqueezeCohort.contextual_regret_cluster` returns at least `minimumFailures` affected projects with each project's regret conclusion. The structure requires that threshold to be at least two; `PriceSqueezeCohort.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` therefore returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's regret conclusion.

## 6. Cohort Conditions and Scope

`CreditShockCohort` requires a finite exposed group of distinct ActionFrame agents and one fiduciary issuance from the common lender. Each collateralized advance must follow that issue on the same history, each borrower and project actor must match its cohort agent, and total cohort principal must fit within the amount issued. The cohort also shares a quoted credit rate, market-signal reader, and response policy; its origination evidence does not require successful repayment. `SavingCreditLoanQuotes` derives equal saving and credit rates from shared Catallactic clearing at a singleton reservation price. Repayment-based comparisons are separate theorems and require explicit repayment witnesses. The saving-side evidence records purposeful retention and proves the principal remains available at the advance. The shared policy then selects the same plan under either observation. Project evidence separately establishes purposeful investment in the credit regime. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or establish saving-side loan execution from the purposeful saving choice alone.

The shared-input and price-squeeze theorems derive a cluster only when their aggregate resource or cash-buffer conditions hold. They do not derive such conditions from fiduciary issuance alone. The one-unit-shortfall boundary witness leaves exactly one of two unit-demand projects unfunded, so a two-project cluster needs a shortfall of at least two. The post-price cash boundary theorem proves completion feasible when the project is contemplated, cash covers its factor bill, and all other inputs are covered. This cash condition is explicit; a price increase alone does not prove that a project's balance falls below the adjusted threshold. The four-funded-project/two-completion-input buffer claim remains unresolved: more cash does not supply the missing physical inputs. The `CreditShockCohort`, `SharedInputShortage`, and `PriceSqueezeCohort` structures range over an existing `ActionFrame`. Saving and credit rate equality can be derived from purposeful clearing in a shared Catallactic market with a singleton mutually acceptable price. A separate repayment-observation result derives equality from matching enacted loan and repayment offers when actual repayment evidence is supplied. Purposeful saving evidence also shows the lender retains enough money through the loan date. This does not trace the balance's original source, identify the loan as financed exclusively by those saved funds, or derive the contract from the saving choice. The general [`CreditShockCohort.requires_three_agents`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L89) theorem proves that two distinct borrowers and their common lender require at least three ActionFrame agents. The bilateral and grounded frames have only two. In the three-agent workshop, the repayment-qualified cohort result [`workshop_repayment_evidence_is_incompatible_with_credit_shock_member`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L192) proves that a credit-cohort member cannot supply repayment evidence; this does not exclude the cohort's origination evidence by itself. The finite profile program supplies a recursive ActionFrame construction for the named schedules; each market clears from the branch state when reached, and assigned terms are not automatically revised afterward.

The independent workshop restriction remains: [`workshop_credit_cohort_cannot_expose_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L161) proves that its enacted exchange pattern cannot support two distinct borrowers sharing one lender. The two-agent exclusions are also proved by [`abct_grounded_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L135) and [`bilateral_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L147). The separate unpaid-maturity witness uses the existing three-agent lifecycle frame. `ProfileProgram` supplies the five-project physical program and an ActionFrame adapter for its schedule, including early input and capital production and contractual maturity outcomes. `BehavioralChoices` proves the selected household plan purposeful under its explicit preference; `BehavioralProgram` and `IntegratedResults` prove subjective purposefulness of project-two and project-three loan acceptance under the separate four-factor forecast. `IntegratedResults` also proves contextual regret for the actual default branch at either marginal borrower node. The forecast is not derived from the actual household choice, and foreclosure remains outside the frame.

## 7. Methodological Boundaries

The implementation does not add universal STIT opportunity or refrainability requirements. It also does not treat “non-martingale filtration” as a formal target. Any future epistemic-dynamics extension needs a stochastic process, a filtration, and a precise statement of which process property is at issue.
