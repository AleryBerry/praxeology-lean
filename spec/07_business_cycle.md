# Specification 07: Business-Cycle Mechanisms and Remaining Construction

This specification describes the current business-cycle modules: [`Entrepreneurship.lean`](../Praxeology/Entrepreneurship.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Information.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Information.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean), and [`BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean).

The results are conditional mechanisms. They do not establish that every credit expansion causes a crisis or that a credit shock alone synchronizes errors across an economy.

## 1. One Entrepreneurial Cycle

`EntrepreneurialCycleScenario` records an entrepreneur's investment, intertemporal rate signals, capital formation, and contextual evaluation. The cycle theorem combines purposeful investment with blocked completion and derives regret from an explicitly feasible safe action, a shared counterfactual context, admitted outcomes, and a valuation comparison. Blockage by itself is insufficient for regret.

`CreditProjectEvidence` packages a cycle with its appraisal and credit-financing menu. Its purposeful-choice theorem uses the existing rate-appraisal and loan-advance conditions. A quoted rate change alone does not determine an entrepreneur's preference or choice.

The finite ActionFrame witness in `BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean` constructs one entrepreneur's matched investment, missing complement, blocked completion, and contextual regret. Its second actor is the exchange counterparty; it is not a two-entrepreneur cluster model.

## 2. Saving and Credit Signals

`CreditShockCohort.observation` reads each regime directly from its `ActionFrame` decision node and Catallactics signal reader; its knowledge relation is equality of those derived observations. The module derives market signals from existing data: loan gross repayment factors, prices in enacted clearing actions, and factor-price bounds. One route to equal saving and credit rate signals is `CatallacticCreditRateComparison`: both contracts' gross repayment factors are tied to purposeful clearing prices in the same Catallactic market, with the same borrower and lender and a singleton mutually acceptable reservation interval. `PurposefulFundedClearingRound.same_price_of_singleton_reservation` derives equal clearing prices from the ActionFrame-grounded reservation brackets; `CatallacticCreditRateComparison.same_gross_repayment_factor` then derives equal contract rates. This is conditional on common-market and singleton-reservation evidence, not on regime labels alone. A separate contract-accounting route applies when the saving-side and credit-side contracts enact the same advance and repayment offers. ActionFrame offer injectivity then yields equal principal and repayment quantities; `PawnLoanTerms.same_grossRepaymentFactor_of_same_principal_and_repayment` in `Catallactics/Credit.lean` derives equal gross repayment factors from the loan identity. Saving evidence also records purposeful retention of a principal-sized balance and no money spent before the advance; the inventory balance law proves that the balance remains available at the loan. The no-shock quote is tied to the natural rate. The ABCT appraisal inequalities prove that the credit rate is lower than that baseline, and the cohort's rate classifier derives the lower-rate observation. Saving and credit observations are equal by theorem rather than by an equality field, and `market_rate_does_not_identify_credit_regime` derives indistinguishability from that result. `source_disclosure_distinguishes_saving_and_credit` shows that an additional source disclosure distinguishes the regimes even when the market reading alone does not; that disclosure is an explicit extra observation, not inferred from rate equality. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or fully establish funding origin.

`ObservationPolicy.same_observation_same_choice` shows that equal observations yield the same policy response under one fixed policy. The result does not establish that the response is available or executed. The credit-project records provide separate appraisal, funding, menu, feasibility, and execution evidence for the credit decision. No saving-regime execution is inferred from signal equality.

## 3. Shared Resource Shortages

`at_least_k_projects_unfunded` proves that if accessible supply falls `k` units below the number of unit-demand projects, at least `k` distinct projects receive no input. The cluster result therefore requires a shortfall bound with `k ≥ 2`.

`SharedInputShortage` ties this accounting to a `CreditShockCohort` and ActionFrame balances. Its finite resource-owner set is partitioned into accessible owners and consumers retaining the input; gross supply, retained stock, and accessible stock are sums of their actual balances. The exposed entrepreneurs' balances are a subset of accessible holdings, so the aggregate allocation bound is derived. Each project must require one unit of the shared input, and the projects must share the relevant completion node. The theorem derives blockage for each zero-allocation project, then composes that blockage with the project's own purposeful-choice and contextual-regret evidence.

`SharedInputShortage.contextual_regret_cluster` packages that composition as a finite witness: at least `minimumFailures` failed projects are returned, each with its entrepreneurial-cycle conclusion. Since the structure requires `minimumFailures ≥ 2`, `SharedInputShortage.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` further returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's cycle conclusion.

The declared resource-owner set must cover the relevant pool, including transfers, production, and imports represented in its ActionFrame balances. The theorem concerns completion at the specified node; it does not prove permanent failure.

## 4. Price Squeezes

`CommonFactorPriceResponse` states a shared price-box change: one bottleneck factor's upper price rises by a specified amount, while other upper prices remain fixed. The module proves the resulting increase in completion cost from factor demand.

`PriceSqueezeCohort` connects the price-box response to ActionFrame observations and cash balances. It ties the pre- and post-response factor-price signals to decision nodes on the same history and proves that the bottleneck price signal changes. The price rule derives higher completion cost; the cash-buffer crossing then derives blockage and, with each project's safe alternative and contextual evaluation, regret. Its `minimumFailures` threshold states how many affected projects the scenario requires. `at_least_minimum_failures_unaffordable` and `contextual_regret_cluster` return a finite subset meeting that bound. `completion_feasible_with_sufficient_post_price_cash` gives the boundary case: if an affected project's completion is contemplated, its cash covers the post-response factor bill, and its other inputs are covered, the ActionFrame feasibility law proves that completion remains feasible.

`PriceSqueezeCohort.contextual_regret_cluster` returns at least `minimumFailures` affected projects with each project's regret conclusion. The structure requires that threshold to be at least two; `PriceSqueezeCohort.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` therefore returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's regret conclusion.

## 5. Cohort Conditions and Scope

`CreditShockCohort` requires a finite exposed group of distinct ActionFrame agents and one fiduciary issuance from the common lender. Each collateralized advance must follow that issue on the same history, each borrower and project actor must match its cohort agent, and total cohort principal must fit within the amount issued. The cohort also shares a quoted credit rate, market-signal reader, and response policy. `SavingCreditLoanQuotes` derives equal saving and credit rates either from shared Catallactic clearing at a singleton reservation price or, when the two loan contracts enact matching advance and repayment offers, from equal principal and repayment quantities. The saving-side evidence records purposeful retention and proves the principal remains available at the advance. The shared policy then selects the same plan under either observation. Project evidence separately establishes purposeful investment in the credit regime. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or establish saving-side loan execution from the purposeful saving choice alone.

The shared-input and price-squeeze theorems derive a cluster only when their aggregate resource or cash-buffer conditions hold. They do not derive such conditions from fiduciary issuance alone. The one-unit-shortfall boundary witness leaves exactly one of two unit-demand projects unfunded, so a two-project cluster needs a shortfall of at least two. The post-price cash boundary theorem proves completion feasible when the project is contemplated, cash covers its factor bill, and all other inputs are covered. The `CreditShockCohort`, `SharedInputShortage`, and `PriceSqueezeCohort` structures all range over an existing `ActionFrame`. Saving and credit rate equality can be derived either from purposeful clearing in a shared Catallactic market with a singleton mutually acceptable price or, separately, from matching enacted loan offers and repayments. Purposeful saving evidence also shows the lender retains enough money through the loan date. This does not trace the balance's original source, identify the loan as financed exclusively by those saved funds, or derive the contract from the saving choice. The general [`CreditShockCohort.requires_three_agents`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean) theorem proves that two distinct borrowers and their common lender require at least three ActionFrame agents. The bilateral and grounded frames have only two; the three-agent workshop cannot host a completed matched loan lifecycle, as [`workshop_no_credit_repayment`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Firms.lean) proves. No new finite world model is added.

The workshop limitation is stronger than the lifecycle result: [`workshop_credit_cohort_cannot_expose_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean) proves that its enacted exchange pattern cannot support two distinct borrowers sharing one lender. The two-agent exclusions are also proved by [`abct_grounded_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean) and [`bilateral_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean). A finite multi-entrepreneur witness still needs an ActionFrame instance with the required issuance, advance, choice, and failure events.

## 6. Methodological Boundaries

The implementation does not add universal STIT opportunity or refrainability requirements. It also does not treat “non-martingale filtration” as a formal target. Any future epistemic-dynamics extension needs a stochastic process, a filtration, and a precise statement of which process property is at issue.
