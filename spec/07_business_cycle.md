# Specification 07: Business-Cycle Mechanisms and Remaining Construction

This specification describes the current business-cycle modules: [`Entrepreneurship.lean`](../Praxeology/Entrepreneurship.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Information.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Information.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Clusters.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean), [`BusinessCycle/CreditLifecycleFrame.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/CreditLifecycleFrame.lean), [`BusinessCycle/DefaultWitness.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/DefaultWitness.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Auctions.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Auctions.lean), [`BusinessCycle/Regimes.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/Regimes.lean), [`BusinessCycle/FactorExecution.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/FactorExecution.lean), and [`BusinessCycle/ProfileProgram.lean`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle/ProfileProgram.lean). The shared schedule compiler is in [`FiniteEconomy/ProfileSchedule.lean`](../Praxeology/FiniteEconomy/ProfileSchedule.lean).

The results are conditional mechanisms. They do not establish that every credit expansion causes a crisis or that a credit shock alone synchronizes errors across an economy.

## 1. One Entrepreneurial Cycle

`EntrepreneurialCycleScenario` records an entrepreneur's investment, intertemporal rate signals, capital formation, and contextual evaluation. The cycle theorem combines purposeful investment with blocked completion and derives regret from an explicitly feasible safe action, a shared counterfactual context, admitted outcomes, and a valuation comparison. Blockage by itself is insufficient for regret.

`CreditProjectEvidence` packages a cycle with its appraisal and credit-financing menu. Its purposeful-choice theorem uses the existing rate-appraisal and loan-advance conditions. A quoted rate change alone does not determine an entrepreneur's preference or choice.

The finite ActionFrame witness in `BusinessCycle/Grounded.lean` constructs one entrepreneur's matched investment, missing complement, blocked completion, and contextual regret. Its second actor is the exchange counterparty; it is not a two-entrepreneur cluster model.

## 2. Credit Lifecycle Outcomes

`PawnLoanTerms` stores the principal and promised repayment separately from later events. `EnactedLoanQuote` contains the terms and an enacted advance only; it does not require a successful repayment. The successful main history in `CreditLifecycleFrame.lean` contains a purposeful advance and a matched purposeful repayment before its stated maturity. `CreditRepayment` is evidence of that later event; it is not inferred from the contract's promised amount.

`DefaultWitness.lean` reuses history eight of the same finite lifecycle frame. The first advance remains purposeful there by `PurposefulAct.bundle_invariant`, which derives invariance across histories sharing the decision moment and enacted action. A second advance is matched before the first repayment decision. At that repayment decision, the entrepreneur idles while the lender accepts; the acceptance is unmatched, so the loan is not repaid. The branch reaches its declared maturity at the resulting endpoint. The proof rules out a matched repayment at every intervening history moment by the enacted actions on the branch. `UnpaidMaturity` derives default status from this evidence. The inventory run records the collateral received in the second advance, proves the lender does not spend collateral before maturity, and derives an aggregate collateral balance at maturity at least as large as the first advance's post-origination balance plus its pledged quantity.

The unpaid branch also supplies the enacted quote `entrepreneurUnpaidEnactedLoanQuote`; `entrepreneur_unpaid_quote_has_no_repayment` proves that its advance has no successful-repayment certificate. This exhibits default without making repayment part of origination. This is an aggregate stock result for a fungible good. The ActionFrame does not track the identity or legal title of particular collateral units, or represent a foreclosure judgment, so the result is not reported as foreclosure. The default branch is also separate from `BusinessCycle.Regimes`; it does not complete the five-project model's causal chain or derive contextual regret.

## 3. Saving and Credit Signals

`CreditShockCohort.observation` reads each regime directly from its `ActionFrame` decision node and Catallactics signal reader; its knowledge relation is equality of those derived observations. The module derives market signals from existing data: loan gross repayment factors, prices in enacted clearing actions, and factor-price bounds. One route to equal saving and credit rate signals is `CatallacticCreditRateComparison`: both contracts' gross repayment factors are tied to purposeful clearing prices in the same Catallactic market, with the same borrower and lender and a singleton mutually acceptable reservation interval. `PurposefulFundedClearingRound.same_price_of_singleton_reservation` derives equal clearing prices from the ActionFrame-grounded reservation brackets; `CatallacticCreditRateComparison.same_gross_repayment_factor` then derives equal contract rates. This is conditional on common-market and singleton-reservation evidence, not on regime labels alone. `SavingCreditLoanQuotes` packages the enacted saving-side and credit-side advances, purposeful saving evidence, and this market comparison; it does not require repayment. Separate theorems comparing two contracts from identical enacted advance and repayment offers take actual `CreditRepayment` evidence for each contract. ActionFrame offer injectivity then yields equal principal and repayment quantities; `PawnLoanTerms.same_grossRepaymentFactor_of_same_principal_and_repayment` in `Catallactics/Credit.lean` derives equal gross repayment factors from the loan identity. Those theorems concern repayment observations and do not make repayment part of loan origination. Saving evidence also records purposeful retention of a principal-sized balance and no money spent before the advance; the inventory balance law proves that the balance remains available at the loan. The no-shock quote is tied to the natural rate. The ABCT appraisal inequalities prove that the credit rate is lower than that baseline, and the cohort's rate classifier derives the lower-rate observation. Saving and credit observations are equal by theorem rather than by an equality field, and `market_rate_does_not_identify_credit_regime` derives indistinguishability from that result. `source_disclosure_distinguishes_saving_and_credit` shows that an additional source disclosure distinguishes the regimes even when the market reading alone does not; that disclosure is an explicit extra observation, not inferred from rate equality. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or fully establish funding origin.

`ObservationPolicy.same_observation_same_choice` shows that equal observations yield the same policy response under one fixed policy. The result does not establish that the response is available or executed. The credit-project records provide separate appraisal, funding, menu, feasibility, and execution evidence for the credit decision. `FactorExecution` runs the five-project household-to-bank cash transfer and factor transfers. The cash transfer is not paired with a deposit claim or a bank repayment obligation, and it does not prove purposeful saving or infer execution from signal equality.

The named household-saving calibration makes an additional cash assumption: the household starts with two money units and transfers both to the bank. The fixed example specifies the household's two completion inputs but not this cash endowment. `fiveProjectRegimeDataWithHouseholdMoney` exposes both endowment and transfer as inputs; the funding result is conditional on those inputs. The current physical ledger records only the cash leg, not a deposit claim or a bank repayment obligation.

The separate five-project calculations in `BusinessCycle.Regimes` make the forecast condition explicit. In the fiduciary case, the funding auction quotes four and the stated lower-rate forecast policy predicts a factor price of one. Under the scenario's terminal-money preference, each funded borrower prefers that forecast plan to idling. Actual factor supply produces price two. The projected factor-demand filter applies the model's one-unit initial-buffer participation rule, so borrowers two and three are excluded at that price. In the executed funding state, each marginal borrower holds two money units and can physically pay the two-unit factor price; `fiveProject_marginal_borrowers_funded_balance_exceeds_buffer_test` exposes the distinction between initial-buffer participation and current physical affordability. This exclusion therefore does not establish physical inability to pay or a price-induced failure in the executed branch. `FactorExecution` executes explicit note issuance, the household cash-transfer leg, funded loan advances, factor transfers, completion operations, and customer sales. `ProfileProgram` recomputes later loan and factor markets from the reached branch state, while retaining the initial-buffer participation rule and separately checking each proposed physical transfer against current inventory. These stages do not establish a deposit claim, purposeful saving, or purposeful investment. The boundary theorem `fiveProject_sufficient_real_inputs_output_sales_execute` changes only supplier stock to four and derives completion and sales by all four funded projects at price one. The fixed case with two completion inputs and four funded projects remains unresolved under larger buffers: the buffer comparison alone cannot provide the missing physical inputs.

`ProfileProgram` places the named regime schedule in a fuel-bounded recursive physical program. `ProfileSchedule` remains the fixed-schedule specialization. The generated program makes each proposed action and each explicitly listed affordable alternative available from the current pre-state; every stage also retains idling. Each branch continues from the state produced by the existing inventory interpreter. In the household-transfer scenario, the household's full factor-consumption operation is an explicit alternative. `fiveProject_household_transfer_schedule_retains_consumption_branch` proves that this alternative remains feasible after the named cash transfer and loan advances. The selected history follows the scheduled profile; menu availability does not prove that idling or transferring cash was purposeful. The generator clears loan capacity from the bank's actual money after the issuance and household-transfer steps. It records an advance only for a matched offer and acceptance. At the later factor-market cursor, it uses those actual advances and currently offered factors, including only the household's stated remaining offer. Each clearing result and its terms remain fixed once assigned; downstream rounds are computed when reached, without automatic reauction. Refused transfers leave inventory unchanged and consume their cursor slot, so they are not retried. `fiveProject_named_program_settlement_balances` checks lender and borrower balances on the named schedules. The fiduciary schedule records the marginal borrowers' two-unit balances and attempts their four-unit promised repayments; neither transfer matches. This does not classify nonpayment as default. The missing deposit claim and repayment obligation, earlier input and capital-production stage, behaviorally derived saving and investment, and foreclosure remain unmodeled.

## 4. Shared Resource Shortages

`at_least_k_projects_unfunded` proves that if accessible supply falls `k` units below the number of unit-demand projects, at least `k` distinct projects receive no input. The cluster result therefore requires a shortfall bound with `k ≥ 2`.

`SharedInputShortage` ties this accounting to a `CreditShockCohort` and ActionFrame balances. Its finite resource-owner set is partitioned into accessible owners and consumers retaining the input; gross supply, retained stock, and accessible stock are sums of their actual balances. The exposed entrepreneurs' balances are a subset of accessible holdings, so the aggregate allocation bound is derived. Each project must require one unit of the shared input, and the projects must share the relevant completion node. The theorem derives blockage for each zero-allocation project, then composes that blockage with the project's own purposeful-choice and contextual-regret evidence.

`SharedInputShortage.contextual_regret_cluster` packages that composition as a finite witness: at least `minimumFailures` failed projects are returned, each with its entrepreneurial-cycle conclusion. Since the structure requires `minimumFailures ≥ 2`, `SharedInputShortage.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` further returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's cycle conclusion.

The declared resource-owner set must cover the relevant pool, including transfers, production, and imports represented in its ActionFrame balances. The theorem concerns completion at the specified node; it does not prove permanent failure.

## 5. Price Squeezes

`CommonFactorPriceResponse` states a shared price-box change: one bottleneck factor's upper price rises by a specified amount, while other upper prices remain fixed. The module proves the resulting increase in completion cost from factor demand.

`PriceSqueezeCohort` connects the price-box response to ActionFrame observations and cash balances. It ties the pre- and post-response factor-price signals to decision nodes on the same history and proves that the bottleneck price signal changes. The price rule derives higher completion cost; the cash-buffer crossing then derives blockage and, with each project's safe alternative and contextual evaluation, regret. Its `minimumFailures` threshold states how many affected projects the scenario requires. `at_least_minimum_failures_unaffordable` and `contextual_regret_cluster` return a finite subset meeting that bound. `completion_feasible_with_sufficient_post_price_cash` gives the boundary case: if an affected project's completion is contemplated, its cash covers the post-response factor bill, and its other inputs are covered, the ActionFrame feasibility law proves that completion remains feasible.

`PriceSqueezeCohort.contextual_regret_cluster` returns at least `minimumFailures` affected projects with each project's regret conclusion. The structure requires that threshold to be at least two; `PriceSqueezeCohort.two_distinct_regretful_entrepreneurs` therefore returns two different cohort members, proves their ActionFrame agents differ, and supplies each project's regret conclusion.

## 6. Cohort Conditions and Scope

`CreditShockCohort` requires a finite exposed group of distinct ActionFrame agents and one fiduciary issuance from the common lender. Each collateralized advance must follow that issue on the same history, each borrower and project actor must match its cohort agent, and total cohort principal must fit within the amount issued. The cohort also shares a quoted credit rate, market-signal reader, and response policy; its origination evidence does not require successful repayment. `SavingCreditLoanQuotes` derives equal saving and credit rates from shared Catallactic clearing at a singleton reservation price. Repayment-based comparisons are separate theorems and require explicit repayment witnesses. The saving-side evidence records purposeful retention and proves the principal remains available at the advance. The shared policy then selects the same plan under either observation. Project evidence separately establishes purposeful investment in the credit regime. The evidence does not trace the original source of the saved balance or establish saving-side loan execution from the purposeful saving choice alone.

The shared-input and price-squeeze theorems derive a cluster only when their aggregate resource or cash-buffer conditions hold. They do not derive such conditions from fiduciary issuance alone. The one-unit-shortfall boundary witness leaves exactly one of two unit-demand projects unfunded, so a two-project cluster needs a shortfall of at least two. The post-price cash boundary theorem proves completion feasible when the project is contemplated, cash covers its factor bill, and all other inputs are covered. This cash condition is explicit; a price increase alone does not prove that a project's balance falls below the adjusted threshold. The four-funded-project/two-completion-input buffer claim remains unresolved: more cash does not supply the missing physical inputs. The `CreditShockCohort`, `SharedInputShortage`, and `PriceSqueezeCohort` structures range over an existing `ActionFrame`. Saving and credit rate equality can be derived from purposeful clearing in a shared Catallactic market with a singleton mutually acceptable price. A separate repayment-observation result derives equality from matching enacted loan and repayment offers when actual repayment evidence is supplied. Purposeful saving evidence also shows the lender retains enough money through the loan date. This does not trace the balance's original source, identify the loan as financed exclusively by those saved funds, or derive the contract from the saving choice. The general [`CreditShockCohort.requires_three_agents`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L73) theorem proves that two distinct borrowers and their common lender require at least three ActionFrame agents. The bilateral and grounded frames have only two. In the three-agent workshop, the repayment-qualified cohort result [`workshop_repayment_evidence_is_incompatible_with_credit_shock_member`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L176) proves that a credit-cohort member cannot supply repayment evidence; this does not exclude the cohort's origination evidence by itself. The finite profile program supplies a recursive ActionFrame construction for the named schedules; each market clears from the branch state when reached, and assigned terms are not automatically revised afterward.

The independent workshop restriction remains: [`workshop_credit_cohort_cannot_expose_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L145) proves that its enacted exchange pattern cannot support two distinct borrowers sharing one lender. The two-agent exclusions are also proved by [`abct_grounded_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L119) and [`bilateral_frame_cannot_host_two_borrowers`](../Praxeology/BusinessCycle.lean#L131). The unpaid-maturity branch uses the existing three-agent lifecycle frame. `ProfileProgram` supplies a finite multi-entrepreneur ActionFrame adapter for the named schedule, including matched funding, factor trades, output sales, and repayment attempts. It does not represent auction bidding as choices, add the missing early-production stage, or connect nonpayment to the separate default and collateral-custody lifecycle.

## 7. Methodological Boundaries

The implementation does not add universal STIT opportunity or refrainability requirements. It also does not treat “non-martingale filtration” as a formal target. Any future epistemic-dynamics extension needs a stochastic process, a filtration, and a precise statement of which process property is at issue.
