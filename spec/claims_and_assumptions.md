# Claim and assumption register

This register distinguishes what Lean proves from what an economic interpretation may claim. A theorem establishes its stated proposition under its explicit hypotheses and the definitions in the imported modules. It does not, by itself, establish that those definitions are a faithful or exhaustive account of an economic theory.

## Claim classes

| Class | What is established | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Definition-level consequence | A result follows from definitions and explicitly stated assumptions. | That the assumptions are universal facts about real agents or institutions. |
| Conditional mechanism | A conclusion follows when a specified technology, menu, preference, information, or institutional condition holds. | That the condition always holds or is caused by another event unless a theorem derives that link. |
| Instantiated witness | A concrete model satisfies the listed predicates and theorem conclusion. | A general result for every economy, or a historically representative case. |
| Countermodel / boundary case | A model shows that a proposed implication fails under the exhibited conditions. | That the failure occurs in all models or that the countermodel is empirically typical. |
| Interpretation | A mathematical structure is read as an economic concept. | That the reading is uniquely compelled by the formal structure. |

## Foundational axioms and dependencies

The repository uses Mathlib for finite sets, order theory, arithmetic, tactics, and other library results. Importing Mathlib is not itself an axiom. Lean's kernel checks completed theorem proofs; the relevant foundational dependency is the transitive axiom set of each declaration. The project currently accepts Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound` dependencies where they occur. This is an explicit foundational choice, not a claim of constructivity. No repository-specific `axiom` or `sorry` is an acceptable substitute for a proof. See `scripts/audit_constructive_axioms.py` for declaration-level dependency reporting.

The `Classical.choice` dependency is standard in ordinary classical mathematics and is not, by itself, evidence of unsoundness. A separate project could pursue a constructive development, but removing Mathlib would not remove choice automatically; choice can be used without Mathlib, and Mathlib theorems may be axiom-free. The question is what the proof depends on, not whether a library is imported.

## Witness requirements

For every headline applied result, documentation should name (1) the instantiated model, (2) the declared agents, times, histories, actions, and means, (3) the preferences and institutional/technological premises, and (4) the theorem conclusion actually proved. A witness of a base `ActionFrame` does not witness a credit market, an exchange network, or a business-cycle cluster. A countermodel should identify the exact implication it refutes and the assumptions it changes.
